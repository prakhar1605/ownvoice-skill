# Match a supplied voice

Read this when the user supplies writing samples or explicitly asks to match
their voice. Two or three short samples can help, but work with what is available.
If there are no samples, draft from the user's stated preferences and context;
do not claim to know their personal style.

Identify a few tendencies that matter for this message:

- **Directness:** Do they get to the request immediately, or briefly establish
  context first?
- **Warmth:** Which acknowledgments, greetings, and closings do they actually use?
- **Rhythm:** Short lines, connected paragraphs, fragments in chat, or fuller
  sentences in email?
- **Vocabulary:** Everyday words, precise technical terms, contractions, and
  familiar code-switching.
- **Register:** Formality, punctuation, emojis, and how they address the recipient.

Use these observations silently unless the user asks for a voice analysis. Match
patterns, not every quirk. A single typo is not a preference. Do not transplant
a sample's people, events, metrics, opinions, promises, signatures, or private
details into the new message unless independently supplied for this task.

Weight samples from a similar channel and relationship more heavily. Casual
WhatsApp samples may show vocabulary preferences, but do not justify calling a
new client `bro`. Respect an explicit request for a different tone over samples.
If samples disagree, choose the relevant register without inventing a single
fixed personality.

For Hinglish, match the user's balance of Hindi and English and their script.
Maintain address level and avoid introducing gendered phrasing without evidence.
Technical words often work best unchanged. If a requested form needs information
that cannot be phrased naturally without a gender assumption, ask briefly.

Use samples only for the current task unless the user explicitly asks to save
preferences and the host supports it. Never write raw private samples into a
public repository. If asked to save a reusable profile, prefer a short summary
of style preferences and use only the authorized destination. Do not claim
persistent memory unless it was actually saved.
